Starting point

Two pictures of one particle

Quantum

$$i\hbar\,\Psi_t = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2M}\Psi_{xx} + V\,\Psi$$
  • A complex wave $\Psi(x,t)$, not a trajectory.
  • $|\Psi|^2$ is the probability density: postulated.
  • Very accurate, and very hard to picture.

Classical

$$M\frac{dx}{dt} = p, \qquad \frac{dp}{dt} = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}$$
  • A point $(x,p)$ moving through phase space.
  • Definite position and momentum at every instant.
  • Fully intuitive.
What must be added to classical mechanics to get quantum mechanics? Surprisingly little: three ideas you already know from math courses.

Phase space: the plane of position and momentum. A classical state is one point in it, and a classical history is a curve.
