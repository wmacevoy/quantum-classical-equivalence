A measurement, watched

Measuring B decoheres the pair, not A

$N$ = 0
  • The pair: $\alpha\,|{\rm cat}_+\rangle_A|L\rangle_B + \beta\,|{\rm cat}_-\rangle_A|R\rangle_B$. The checkerboard in $P(p_A,p_B)$ is the A–B interference between the two branches.
  • $N$ pointer modes touch only B. Each kicks $p_B$, so the joint picture is smeared sideways only and the fringes wash out.
  • A's own distribution is identical for every $N$. The coherence lived in the pair, and the kick spreads it into the apparatus.
L–R coherence $\;e^{-N g^2 \Delta x_B^2 / 8\sigma^2}$ 1.000

Same state and parameters as the simulation in the paper: packets of width 0.5, cats and L/R separated by 4, α² = 0.7, g = 1, single-pointer width σ = 6. One pointer cannot resolve B's branches (coherence 0.95); by N = 64 it is 0.03.

The picture is exact: the kick pB → pB − gPtot is a Gaussian convolution in pB of variance N g²/4σ², done here in closed form. Integrating over pB kills the fringe term at every N, which is why A's marginal on the left never moves.

The back-action is the uncertainty principle: a sharp collective pointer position means a broad Ptot, hence a broad kick. A point pointer would measure without disturbing, but it is not a quantum state.
