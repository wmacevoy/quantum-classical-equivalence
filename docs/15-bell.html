Mystery 3, part two

The causality puzzle Bell left us

  • Alice and Bob share a pair. Bob measures. The textbook says Alice's state collapses instantly, however far away she is.
  • Bell (1964): no local model built on ordinary (non-negative) hidden-variable probabilities reproduces all quantum correlations.
  • Experiments agree with quantum mechanics, including the loophole-free tests of 2015 (Nobel Prize 2022: Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger).
  • So: does something travel faster than light? The usual menu is to give up locality, give up realism, or accept many worlds.

The looking-glass reading

  • Dynamics stays local. Each particle's $w$ flows by its own Hamiltonian.
  • Collapse is conditioning on a pointer reading. Nothing is sent.
  • What Bell forbids, a non-negative joint distribution, is exactly what we don't have: $w$ is signed.

To check that this isn't hand-waving, we model the measurement itself as von Neumann did: a physical pointer interacting with B.

This is the heart of the talk. The causality question: when Bob measures, does anything happen at Alice's location? The next three slides answer with a concrete calculation where every step is exact.
