The entire difference is one term

To make the centered difference exact, the classical equation would need one more term:

$$w_t + \frac{p}{M}\,w_x - \frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\,w_p \;=\; \underbrace{-\,\frac{\hbar^2}{24}\,\frac{\partial^3 V}{\partial x^3}\,\frac{\partial^3 w}{\partial p^3}}_{\text{leading Moyal correction}} \;+\;\cdots$$
  • Exact when $V''' = 0$: free particles, uniform fields, harmonic oscillators, and any quadratic coupling. There, classical flow of $w$ is quantum mechanics.
  • Otherwise it's locally small ($\hbar^2$), but it accumulates. In anharmonic wells classical clouds filament, while quantum states interfere and revive.
  • Where the two disagree, experiment follows quantum mechanics. So this is not a claim that classical mechanics is fundamental.
  • The claim is structural: the whole dynamical gap between probabilistic classical mechanics and quantum mechanics is this term. It vanishes in the setups we'll use for entanglement.

Keep the last bullet in mind. For free particles and a linear coupling to a measuring device, every step is exact, so nothing on the entanglement slides is an approximation.
