A measurement, modeled

Measuring B with a classical pointer

  • Free particles A and B, and a pointer with position $X$ and momentum $P$, coupled only to B: $H_{\rm int} = g(t)\,x_B\,P$.
  • Every Hamiltonian is at most quadratic, so the transform is exact. What follows is literally classical Liouville flow.
$$X \to X + g\,x_B \quad\text{(pointer records B)}, \qquad p_B \to p_B - g\,P \quad\text{(B is kicked)}$$
Integrate out B and the pointer: A's distribution is unchanged. The flow is local.

Von Neumann's model of measurement: an interaction that makes a pointer's position depend on the measured quantity. Hamilton's equations for H = g xB P give exactly the two shifts shown.

After the interaction the joint distribution is wAB(xA, pA, xB, pB + gP) · wptr(X − g xB, P). Nothing in it acts on A.
