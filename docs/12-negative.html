The first objection

“Wait, $w$ goes negative?” Like a pressure wave

  • The Liouville equation is about flux. If $w$ solves it, so does $w + c$: a uniform background has no gradient and carries no flow.
  • Acoustic pressure works the same way. The perturbation is negative in every rarefaction, and nobody minds.
  • For every pure state $-\frac{1}{\pi\hbar} \le w \le \frac{1}{\pi\hbar}$, so $w = \rho - \frac{1}{\pi\hbar}$ with $\rho \ge 0$: an ambient level of one count per quantum cell.
  • Every prediction is a marginal, and every marginal is non-negative. Intermediate quantities need not be probabilities (Feynman, 1987).
  • The negative regions are real and have been measured (Leibfried et al., 1996). By Hudson's theorem they mark exactly the non-Gaussian states.

Everyone's mind goes here, so answer it once and plainly. Negative values aren't missing probability; they are rarefactions below an ambient level. The model is judged by its predictions, and those are all non-negative.

The background has no finite total over infinite phase space, but neither does the ambient pressure of an unbounded medium; it carries no dynamics and enters no prediction.
