Heisenberg, from this side

A point is allowed, but it isn't a quantum state

  • The transformed dynamics happily evolves $w_0 = \delta(x)\,\delta(p)$: a particle at rest at the bottom of a symmetric well.
  • Its shadow is exactly $\delta(x)$, which is what you'd expect.
  • But its coefficient matrix is
    $$C_{\lambda\lambda'} = 2\,\sigma_\lambda\,\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}, \qquad \sigma_\lambda = \pm 1 \text{ (parity)}$$
    and its eigenvalues alternate in sign. $C$ is not positive, so this is not a density matrix.

The uncertainty principle, rephrased

Quantum states are the ones with positive $C$. They form an island inside a larger state space that also contains every classical point preparation.

The dynamics never leaves the island once you're on it.

Why nature only prepares states on the island is an open question.

This is the honest version of "the uncertainty principle is just fuzziness." It isn't only fuzziness: it's a positivity condition on C. The pointer on slide 19 needs this: a perfectly sharp pointer would measure without disturbing, and positivity is what forces back-action.
