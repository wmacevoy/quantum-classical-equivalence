Mystery 2: measurement

Collapse is conditioning

Reading the pointer means taking its $X$-marginal, the same operation as the Born shadow. Condition on the reading $X_0$:

$$w_A(x_A,p_A \mid X_0) \;\propto\; \int dx_B\;\rho_{\rm ptr}(X_0 - g\,x_B)\int dp_B\; w_{AB}(x_A,p_A,x_B,p_B)$$
  • This is exactly the Wigner function of A's quantum state after the measurement on B.
  • Average over the readings and you recover A's original distribution: nothing done at B signals to A. In the simulation they agree to $7\times 10^{-17}$.
The "instant change" at A is a change in what we condition on, not in A's dynamics. Open one of two sealed envelopes: the other envelope doesn't change, only your description of it does.

ρptr is the pointer's initial position density. In the sharp-pointer limit this reduces to the standard identity: the Wigner function of A given xB is the joint w marginalized over pB and evaluated at xB.

The envelope analogy is classical, with non-negative weights. The next slide is the twist: the same formula with signed weights.
