Mystery 3, part one

Entanglement: two dots, one hand

Two cats, two walls. Each dot wanders unpredictably, but the two always move together. Nothing passes between the walls.

  • Hold two laser pointers in one hand and aim them at opposite walls. Each cat sees a random dot. Comparing notes, they'd find the dots perfectly correlated: spooky?
  • We know the secret: a common source, not a signal.
  • In the looking-glass view, an entangled pair is a joint distribution that doesn't factor:
    $$w(x_A,p_A,x_B,p_B) \;\ne\; w_A(x_A,p_A)\;w_B(x_B,p_B)$$
    Each particle flows under its own Hamiltonian. The dependence is in the initial data.
But careful: Bell proved the hand can't be an ordinary, non-negative probability. That is the next slide.

In the paper this is the non-factorizability of the Bell-state coefficient vector, e.g. (|αβ⟩ ± |βα⟩)/√2. Wetterich and Ozorio de Almeida have related readings.

The laser analogy is a classical common-cause story, which is exactly what Bell's theorem constrains. So don't oversell it; turn straight to Bell.
