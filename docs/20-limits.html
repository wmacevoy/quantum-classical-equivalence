What this view does not do

  • It doesn't replace quantum mechanics. For anharmonic potentials over long times the dropped term matters, and experiment follows quantum.
  • It only covers marginal measurements: position, momentum, quadratures. General observables (spin components, parity) need overlaps with Wigner symbols that are themselves signed.
  • No spin, and no boson/fermion rule. Pauli exclusion is representable but not explained.
  • No irreversible record. The pointer here is reversible in principle; amplification isn't modeled.
  • Why positive $C$? Why nature only prepares states on the quantum island is open.
  • The math is known. Wigner, Moyal, Case (2008), and Carnovali & França (2005) did the derivation. The contribution is the reading.

Invite questions here. The marginal-measurement limit is the most important for the Bell discussion: photon-polarization Bell tests use spin-like observables, which this treatment doesn't cover directly. The continuous-variable Bell tests (Nha & Carmichael; García-Patrón et al.) are the ones that fit.
