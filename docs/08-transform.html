The looking-glass transform

Compose the three ideas

1Liouville:   $w_t + \frac{p}{M}w_x - V'(x)\,w_p = 0$

2Fourier transform in $p$, then multiply by $i\hbar$:   $i\hbar\,\hat w_t - \frac{\hbar^2}{M}\,\hat w_{x\hat p} + \hat p\,V'(x)\,\hat w = 0$

↔Change variables to $z_\pm = x \pm \hat p/2$:   $i\hbar\,\hat w_t - \frac{\hbar^2}{2M}\big[\partial_{z_+}^2 - \partial_{z_-}^2\big]\hat w + \hat p\,V'(x)\,\hat w = 0$

3The only approximation, the centered difference with $\varepsilon = \hat p$:   $\hat p\,V'(x) \approx V(z_+) - V(z_-)$

$$i\hbar\,\hat w_t = \big(H_{z_-} - H_{z_+}\big)\,\hat w, \qquad H_z = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2M}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial z^2} + V(z)$$
One Schrödinger Hamiltonian acting on $z_-$, and its mirror image acting on $z_+$.

Every step but the centered difference is exact. The two new coordinates z± are a position shifted forward and backward by half of the Fourier variable.

For anyone who knows more QM: this is the von Neumann equation for a density matrix in position representation, iħρ̇ = [H, ρ].
