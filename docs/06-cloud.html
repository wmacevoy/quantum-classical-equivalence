Idea 1, in action

Follow a cloud, not a point

If we don't know the starting point exactly, we follow a density $w(x,p,t)$ of possible starting points. Each point obeys Newton; the cloud obeys the Liouville equation from the last slide:

$$w_t + \frac{p}{M}\,w_x - \frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\,w_p = 0$$
  • It is a linear PDE even when Newton's ODE is nonlinear.
  • Look at the strip on the right: the cloud's shadow on the $x$ wall is the position distribution.

2,500 Newtonian particles. Harmonic: rigid rotation. Quartic: orbits run at different rates, so the cloud shears into filaments.

This idea goes back to Liouville; Fritz John's PDE text presents it as lifting an ODE to a PDE for the distribution of initial values.

Show both potentials. The harmonic case will matter later: there, classical and quantum evolution of w are identical. The quartic filamentation is where they disagree (slide 10).
